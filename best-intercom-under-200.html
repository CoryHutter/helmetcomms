Best motorcycle intercom under $200

Two hundred dollars used to mean Bluetooth and nothing else. It doesn't anymore — and that single fact should drive your whole decision.

The short version

If you ride with three or more people, buy the Sena Spider ST1 or Spider RT1 at around $179. They're the only genuine mesh units under $200, and mesh is the thing that makes group riding work. If it's just you and a passenger, the Cardo Spirit HD at $179.95 or Sena 5S at around $127 are better buys, because you don't need mesh at all.

The one thing that decides it

Under $200 you're choosing between mesh and everything else, and the answer depends only on how many people you ride with.

Bluetooth units pair in a chain. When somebody in the middle stops for fuel, everyone behind them goes quiet until it's rebuilt by hand. With two riders that never happens. With four it happens constantly.

Mesh has no chain and heals itself. The full explanation is here, but the rule is simple: three or more riders, buy mesh.

Best mesh under $200

Sena Spider ST1 — around $179

Full Mesh 3.0, 24-rider Group Mesh, nine channels for splitting large rides, 1.6 km claimed range, 11 hours of talk time. Jog dial control. This is the cheapest genuine mesh unit either brand sells. Full review.

Sena Spider RT1 — around $179

Identical mesh in a low-profile body with buttons instead of a jog dial. Buy this one if you ride in cold weather or off-road, where a wheel is hard work through thick gloves.

Nothing from Cardo makes it under $200 with mesh. The Packtalk Neo is the cheapest Cardo mesh and it sits around $252 on sale. If your group runs Cardo, that $70 gap is unavoidable — a cheaper Sena that can't join their network is not a saving.

Best Bluetooth under $200

Cardo Spirit HD — $179.95

40 mm HD speakers, 13 hours of talk time, and Cardo's voice control at the bottom of the range. The best-sounding unit at this price and the one to buy if you and your passenger listen to music.

Sena 5S — around $127

Harman Kardon speakers at a hundred and twenty seven dollars, four-rider Bluetooth, 11 hours of talk time. The value pick of this whole page. If mesh isn't a requirement, it's hard to argue with.

Cardo Freecom 2X — around $179

JBL 40 mm speakers and 13 hours, built purely for two riders. Good, but the Spirit HD does much the same for the same money and the 5S undercuts both.

Sena SF4 — around $172

Four riders on Bluetooth with 1.2 km claimed range, which is long for the price. Worth a look if you have a small group but genuinely can't stretch to mesh.

Rock bottom

If the budget is truly tight and you just want to stop shouting at your passenger:

  • Cardo Spirit — $109.95. Cardo's entry unit, 13 hours, does the job.
  • Sena 5R — around $104. Two-way Bluetooth with HD speakers.
  • Sena 5R Lite — around $82. The cheapest thing either brand makes that's worth buying.

All three do intercom, phone calls and music. You give up range, refinement and speaker quality. For a passenger who rides twice a month, that's a reasonable trade.

Two things to check before you order

Watch the sales. Both brands discount heavily and often. Sena has been running 25% off much of its line and Cardo 30% off parts of its. Units that sit above $200 at list frequently fall under it — the Packtalk Neo and the Sena 50S both have.

Check what your group runs. A $179 Sena that can't join your friends' Cardo mesh is worse value than a $252 Neo that can. The networks don't connect.
