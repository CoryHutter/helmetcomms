Sena 60S review

Sena's flagship, and the only intercom that solves the range problem instead of just measuring it. Whether that's worth $155 over the 50S depends on one question.

The short version

$439. Mesh 3.0, Harman Kardon audio, IPX7 waterproofing, 16 hours of mesh talk time and a 1.5-hour charge. The feature that justifies it is WAVE — a cellular intercom channel with no range limit at all. If you regularly get separated from your group by more than a mile, buy it. If you don't, the Sena 50S runs the identical mesh network for around $155 less.

WAVE is the whole argument

Every other intercom on the market, from either brand, is a radio. Radios have range limits and terrain beats them.

WAVE routes intercom through the cellular network instead. Once you're both connected, distance stops mattering — you can talk to a rider an hour ahead of you, or to someone who took a different route entirely and will meet you at the hotel.

That's genuinely new, and it's the most interesting thing either company has shipped in years. It's also useless to a lot of riders. If your group stays within sight of each other, you'll never touch it.

Be honest with yourself about which one you are. The riders who need WAVE know it already — they're the ones who've spent years pulling over at intersections to wait.

Mesh 3.0

The 60S runs Sena's current mesh. Group Mesh handles 24 riders, Open Mesh runs six channels with effectively no participant cap, 2 km claimed range in open terrain.

Sena pushed Mesh 3.0 to the 50S, 50R and several older models by firmware, so this isn't exclusive to the flagship. The 60S can also drop back to Mesh 2.0 to connect with anyone still running the older standard, which the newer 3.0-only behaviour otherwise prevents.

Worth being clear: the mesh on this unit is not better than the mesh on a 50S. It's the same network. You're paying for WAVE, battery and charging speed.

Battery and charging — the real strength

  • Mesh intercom: 16 hours
  • Bluetooth intercom: 22 hours
  • Full charge: 1.5 hours
  • Fast charge: 20 minutes buys about 3.5 hours of mesh

This is a real upgrade over the 50S, which manages 13 hours of mesh and takes 2.5 hours to charge. Sixteen hours outlasts anything you'll ride in a day, and recovering 3.5 hours from a lunch stop is genuinely useful on a long tour.

If you do multi-day trips with early starts and late finishes, this matters more than the spec gap suggests.

The small stuff

A built-in flashlight, which sounds like a gimmick until you're looking for something in a dark parking lot with a helmet on. And a side-mounted LED light bar that shows mode and volume at a glance, and can be switched off to save battery if you'd rather not have it.

IPX7 waterproofing is published, unlike the 50S, which has no stated rating.

60S or 60S EVO?

Sena now sells a 60S EVO at $459 — twenty dollars more. It swaps Harman Kardon for Bose, moves to Bluetooth 5.3, and pushes battery to 19 hours on mesh and 27 on Bluetooth. It also carries a limited lifetime warranty and ships with six interchangeable faceplates.

For twenty dollars, the EVO is the better buy on paper. The only reason to take the standard 60S is if you specifically prefer Harman Kardon's tuning, or if the EVO is out of stock — both units move in and out of availability on Sena's own store.

What you're not getting

No crash detection. That's a Cardo Packtalk Pro feature and Sena doesn't match it at any price. If riding alone is your concern rather than range, the Pro is the more relevant flagship.

No magnetic mount. Cardo's Air Mount remains the nicest hardware detail in the category and Sena has no answer to it.

Jog dial. Fast in summer gloves, awkward in winter ones. Sena has no button-based unit at this tier — the 50R is the button option and it sits a tier down.

Who should buy it

  • Buy the 60S or 60S EVO if you ride long distances with a group that spreads out, or if you tour and value the fast charge.
  • Buy the Sena 50S instead if your group stays together. Same mesh, around $155 less. Full review.
  • Buy the Cardo Packtalk Pro instead if you ride alone and safety is the worry rather than range. Comparison here.
  • Buy Cardo, full stop, if your riding group already runs it. The networks don't connect.
