Cardo Packtalk Edge review

The unit most riders should buy, and the one the rest of the Cardo lineup gets measured against. Here's what you're paying for and where the money actually goes.

The short version

$439.95 direct. Cardo's second-generation mesh, JBL 40 mm speakers, magnetic mount, 13 hours of talk time and an IP67 waterproof rating. The Packtalk Pro above it adds crash detection and bigger speakers for $60. The Packtalk Neo below it runs the identical mesh network for around $190 less but drops the magnetic mount. The Edge is the balance point.

The mount is the actual feature

Most reviews lead with audio or mesh. The thing you'll notice every single day is the Air Mount.

The unit is held on by magnets. Bring it near the base plate and it pulls itself into position and locks. Press a tab, slide forward, and it's off in your hand — one motion, doable with gloves on.

That sounds like a small thing until you've spent a week on the road. Taking the unit off to charge it at a hotel is a two-second job instead of a wrestling match with your helmet, and if you move one unit between a street helmet and a dirt helmet, you just buy a second base plate.

This is the single clearest reason to pay up over the Packtalk Neo, which runs the same mesh and the same speakers but uses a conventional clamp. On the Neo you pull the entire mount off the helmet to charge.

Mesh: what you get and what just changed

The Edge runs Cardo's second-generation Dynamic Mesh — the same network as the Packtalk Pro, Packtalk Neo and Packtalk Edge ORV. Group them once and they find each other on every ride afterwards. Riders drop out and rejoin with nobody touching anything.

Rider-to-rider range is claimed at 1.6 km, about a mile, in open terrain. With hills or buildings in the way you'll see a fraction of that. In a larger group the effective span is much greater than the two-rider figure because riders in the middle relay the signal.

Worth knowing: Cardo released a free over-the-air update called Mesh Boost that raises group capacity substantially past the old 15-rider figure and adds public mesh and remote grouping. It's available to all second-generation DMC units through the Cardo Connect app, so an Edge bought today gets it. Older Packtalk generations can still connect to Mesh Boost users, they just don't get the new capabilities themselves.

Sound

JBL 40 mm drivers, with three audio profiles you switch in the app. For intercom it's more than enough. For music at highway speed it's good, and the Packtalk Pro's 45 mm speakers are audibly better if that's what you care about.

The bigger variable is your helmet. Deep ear pockets and a quiet shell will do more for your audio than any speaker upgrade. If your helmet has shallow pockets, look at the slim speaker option before you assume the unit is the problem.

Voice control

Say "Hey Cardo" and tell it what you want. No button, no wheel, no taking a hand off the bar.

When conditions are good this is the best interface on any intercom. When you're wearing earplugs behind a short screen at 75 mph, it's less reliable, and you'll fall back to the control roller. That's not a defect so much as physics — every voice system on a motorcycle deals with it.

If you ride in heavy gloves or cold weather often, this is the point where Sena's button-based units become a genuine alternative.

Battery and charging

  • Talk time: 13 hours
  • Standby: 10 days
  • Full charge: around 2 hours
  • Fast charge: 20 minutes gets you 2 hours of talk time
  • Port: USB-C, and you can ride while it charges

Thirteen hours outlasts any realistic day of riding. The fast charge is the number that matters in practice — plug in over lunch and you've bought back the afternoon.

One note from long-term owners: the USB-C port sits under a rubber weather plug that's fiddly to open with the unit mounted. Minor, but you'll notice it.

Durability and warranty

IP67 waterproof, which is a published rating rather than a vague claim — worth pointing out because Cardo doesn't publish IP ratings on the cheaper Freecom and Spirit lines. Three-year warranty, which is longer than most of the category.

Who should buy it

  • Buy the Edge if you ride with a group, want mesh, and will be taking the unit on and off helmets regularly.
  • Buy the Packtalk Pro instead if you often ride alone or somewhere remote — crash detection with automatic emergency alerts is a real reason to spend the extra $60. Full comparison here.
  • Buy the Packtalk Neo instead if you'll leave the unit on one helmet and charge it in the garage. Same mesh, same speakers, meaningfully cheaper.
  • Don't buy any of them if it's just you and a passenger. You don't need mesh — here's why.
  • Don't buy Cardo at all if your riding group already runs Sena. The two mesh networks don't connect. Details here.
Worth knowing: Cardo has an arrangement with Cycle Gear for free installation with an online purchase. If you're not confident about routing speaker wires and positioning a microphone, that's a real saving on a job that's fiddlier than it looks.
