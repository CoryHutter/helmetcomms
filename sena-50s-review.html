Sena 50S review

The best-value mesh unit either brand sells. Same network as Sena's flagship, frequently under $300, and the jog dial is either the best control on the market or the worst — depending entirely on your gloves.

The short version

$379 list, and Sena discounts it hard — it's been sitting around $284. You get Mesh 3.0, the same network the $439 flagship runs, Harman Kardon audio, 13 hours of mesh talk time and 2 km of claimed range. The only things the 60S adds over it are WAVE, longer battery and faster charging. For most riders that's not $155 of difference.

Same mesh as the flagship

This is the argument for the 50S in one line. Mesh 3.0 on the 50S is the same Mesh 3.0 on the 60S. Group Mesh handles 24 riders, Open Mesh runs six channels with effectively no participant limit, and it self-heals when riders drop out and come back.

Sena pushed Mesh 3.0 to the 50S and 50R by firmware, so a unit bought today is on the current standard. That matters more than it sounds — Mesh 3.0 and the older Mesh 2.0 don't talk to each other, so a group running mixed versions has to drop everyone back to 2.0 to stay connected.

Claimed range is 2 km, which is longer than Cardo publishes for the Packtalk line. Treat both numbers as open-terrain best cases.

The jog dial

Sena's signature control is a wheel you spin and click. Volume, track skip, intercom, all of it through one physical thing you can find without looking.

In summer gloves it's excellent — faster and more certain than hunting for the right button, and far more reliable than voice control in wind. It gives you clear tactile feedback so you know the input registered.

In winter gauntlets or off-road gloves it's a genuine problem. A wheel needs finer motor control than a button, and thick insulation takes that away. Sena clearly knows this, because the 50R is the same unit with buttons instead, at a similar price.

Pick between the 50S and 50R on this basis alone. It's the only meaningful difference between them apart from a couple of hours of battery.

Sound

Harman Kardon speakers, and they're good. Sena and Cardo have been trading audio partnerships for years and neither is meaningfully behind — you'd have to A/B them in the same helmet to call it.

Nine FM channels are built in if you still use radio. And Sena sells a WiFi charging cable that updates firmware automatically when you plug it in, which removes the most annoying part of owning one of these.

Battery and charging — read the right number

Sena publishes two talk-time figures because mesh draws more power than Bluetooth:

  • Bluetooth intercom: 16 hours
  • Mesh intercom: 13 hours
  • Full charge: 2.5 hours
  • Quick charge: 20 minutes gets you about 2 hours

If you're buying this for group rides, 13 hours is your number, not 16. It's still more than a day's riding.

The 2.5-hour full charge is the slowest in Sena's current mesh lineup — the 60S does it in 1.5. Not a dealbreaker when you're charging overnight, worth knowing if you're not.

A caution on Sena's published specs. Their store, product pages and PDF manuals list different talk times for this same unit. The figures above come from Sena's own product spec sheet. If you see a different number elsewhere, that's why — not because someone got it wrong.

What's missing

No published IP rating. Sena rates the 60S at IPX7 and Cardo rates the Packtalks at IP67, but the 50S has no published figure. It's not a fragile unit and riders use them in rain constantly, but the absence of a number is worth noting when you're comparing spec sheets.

No WAVE. The cellular intercom channel with no range limit is a 60-series feature. If you regularly get separated from your group by more than a mile, that's the reason to spend up.

No crash detection. Neither brand offers it at this price — that's a Cardo Packtalk Pro feature.

50S vs the alternatives

  • vs Sena 60S ($439): the 60S adds WAVE, three more hours of mesh talk time and a faster charge. Worth it only if WAVE solves a problem you actually have.
  • vs Sena 50R ($375 list): buttons instead of a jog dial, two hours less talk time, better fast charge. Buy the 50R if you ride in cold weather or off-road.
  • vs Sena Spider ST1 ($239 list): the ST1 is the cheap door into the same mesh. You give up Harman Kardon audio. If budget is the binding constraint, it's the smarter buy.
  • vs Cardo Packtalk Edge ($439.95): the real head-to-head. The Edge has the better mount and voice control; the 50S is usually $150 cheaper. If your group already runs one brand, that settles it.

Who should buy it

Riders who want mesh without paying flagship money, ride in conditions where a jog dial works, and don't need WAVE. That's a lot of people, which is why this unit has stayed at the top of Sena's range for years despite newer models arriving above it.
