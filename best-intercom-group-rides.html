Best motorcycle intercom for group rides

Group riding is the one situation where the wrong intercom actively ruins the day. Here's what to buy, sorted by how many of you there are and what you're willing to spend.

The short version

Buy mesh, not Bluetooth. Then buy whatever brand your group already runs, because Cardo and Sena mesh networks don't connect to each other. Starting fresh: the Cardo Packtalk Edge is the best all-round choice and the Sena 50S is the best value. For a large club, Sena's channel system gives you something Cardo doesn't.

Rule one: mesh, not Bluetooth

Bluetooth intercoms pair in a fixed chain. When the rider in the middle stops for fuel or drops out of range, everyone behind them goes silent, and somebody has to pull over and rebuild the pairing by hand.

With two riders this never comes up. With five it comes up at every stop, and by the third one you'll have given up on the intercom entirely.

Mesh has no chain. Every unit relays for the others, the network reroutes itself as riders move, and people leave and rejoin without anyone pressing anything. That's the entire reason it costs more, and for group riding it's worth it. The longer explanation is here.

Rule two: your group already decided this

Cardo's Dynamic Mesh and Sena's Mesh Intercom are proprietary and closed to each other. A Packtalk cannot join a Sena mesh group.

So if four riders in your crew are on Sena, buy Sena. A cheaper unit on the group's network beats a better unit that can't join it, every time. The workarounds for mixed groups are here, and they work, but they're workarounds.

Three to six riders

Best overall: Cardo Packtalk Edge — $439.95

Second-generation DMC, magnetic mount, JBL 40 mm speakers, 13 hours of talk time and IP67 waterproofing. Cardo's free Mesh Boost update raised group capacity well past the old 15-rider limit and added public mesh and remote grouping, so headroom isn't a concern.

The voice control is the reason it's the pick for group riding specifically. You'll be talking constantly, and "Hey Cardo" beats hunting for a control at 70 mph. Full review.

Best value: Sena 50S — around $284 on sale

Mesh 3.0, the same network Sena's flagship runs. Group Mesh handles 24 riders, 2 km claimed range, Harman Kardon audio, 13 hours of mesh talk time. Regularly $150 less than the Edge.

The jog dial is fast and certain in summer gloves and awkward in winter ones. If your group rides cold, look at the 50R instead — same unit with buttons. Full review.

Cheapest way to get everyone on mesh: Sena Spider ST1 — around $179 on sale

If you're outfitting a whole group at once, the maths change. Six Packtalk Edges is $2,640. Six Spider ST1s is roughly $1,075 and puts everyone on the same self-healing mesh. You give up Harman Kardon audio and some polish. The network works identically.

Large club rides, ten and up

Sena, for the channels

This is the one place Sena has a clear structural advantage. Open Mesh runs six channels with effectively no participant limit, and Sena's Spider units expand that further.

Channels matter at scale for a reason that has nothing to do with capacity: twenty people on one channel is unusable. Nobody can finish a sentence. Splitting road captains onto one channel, sweep riders onto another, and general chat onto a third turns chaos into something workable.

The Spider ST1 gives you nine channels at the lowest price in Sena's mesh range, which makes it the natural club unit.

Cardo, if the club is already on Cardo

Mesh Boost substantially raised DMC group capacity and added remote grouping, so capacity is no longer the constraint it was. Cardo doesn't offer the same channel-splitting structure, so for very large rides you're managing the "everyone talking at once" problem socially rather than technically.

Off-road and adventure groups

Two things change when you leave pavement.

Gloves. A jog dial is hard work in gauntlets. Choose buttons — Sena's 50R or Spider RT1 — or Cardo's voice control, which works fine when you're not doing highway speed.

Terrain. Published range figures assume open ground. In a canyon or under tree cover, expect a fraction of it. This is where mesh earns its money: riders spread out along a trail relay the signal to each other, so the group holds together far past what any two units manage alone.

For UTV, ATV and snowmobile groups, Cardo's Packtalk Edge ORV puts the controls on the handlebar, which is the right answer when you're wearing heavy gloves and can't reach your helmet easily.

What not to buy for group riding

  • Any Bluetooth-only unit. Cardo Freecom, Sena SF4, Sena 5S. All fine products, all wrong for a group of three or more.
  • Sena Spider X Slim, if anyone in your group runs Cardo. It's mesh-only with no Bluetooth intercom at all, so there's no fallback and no bridge. Nothing connects it to a Cardo.
  • Mixed brands, if you can avoid it. It works over Bluetooth for two riders. It doesn't scale to a group.
Before your next big ride: have everyone update their firmware. Sena's Mesh 3.0 and Mesh 2.0 don't talk to each other, and one rider on old firmware drags the whole group back to the older standard. Ten minutes in the app the night before saves an argument in a parking lot.
