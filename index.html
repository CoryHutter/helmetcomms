Mesh or Bluetooth?

This is the only spec that changes how a group ride actually feels. Everything else is preference.

Mesh

Every unit is a relay. Riders drop out and rejoin on their own, and the group heals itself when someone falls behind or shuts down. No pairing chain to rebuild at the gas stop.

  • Worth it from three riders up
  • Range extends as the group grows — riders in the middle pass the signal along
  • Costs more, and only talks to the same brand's mesh

Bluetooth

Units pair to each other in a fixed chain. If a rider in the middle drops off, everyone behind them goes quiet until it's rebuilt by hand.

  • Fine for two riders, or rider and passenger
  • Works across brands — Cardo talks to Sena this way
  • Half the price of a mesh unit
The mixed-brand reality: a Cardo and a Sena can connect, but only over Bluetooth, and only two at a time. Neither one joins the other's mesh. If your regular group has already committed to a brand, that decision is effectively made for you.

Will it fit your helmet?

Fitment kills more of these purchases than price does. Three things to check before you order.

  • Speaker pocket depth. Shallow ear pockets press 40 mm and 45 mm speakers into your ears on hour three. Some helmets need the slim speaker option.
  • Mount type. Clamp mounts need a gap between shell and liner, which some tight-fitting helmets don't have. Adhesive mounts work anywhere but commit you to that helmet.
  • Built-in comms cavity. Shoei, HJC, Schuberth and others have a dedicated pocket that only accepts a matched unit — Cardo's Packtalk-S and 4X-S for Shoei, Sena's SRL2. Cleaner install, no universal option.
