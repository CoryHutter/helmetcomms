Cardo vs Sena: which one should you actually buy?

Both brands make good hardware. The differences that matter aren't the ones on the spec sheet, and the deciding factor usually has nothing to do with either company.

The short version

Buy whatever your regular riding group already runs. The two mesh networks are closed to each other, so the brand decision is really a group decision. If nobody in your crew has committed yet, the Cardo Packtalk Edge and the Sena 50S are the two to look at — and the 50S is currently a lot cheaper.

The mesh networks don't talk to each other

This is the single most important thing to understand, and it's the thing most comparisons bury at the bottom.

Cardo's mesh is called Dynamic Mesh Communication. Sena's is called Mesh Intercom. They are entirely separate systems. A Packtalk cannot join a Sena mesh group and a 50S cannot join a Cardo one. There's no firmware update coming that changes this.

You can connect a Cardo to a Sena over plain Bluetooth intercom, two riders at a time, and on a Cardo Packtalk you can bridge one Bluetooth rider into a mesh group as a guest. It works. It's also clearly a workaround, and everybody in the group knows it. Full details on mixing the two brands here.

So if four of your riding buddies are on Packtalks, buy a Packtalk. The audio comparison below stops mattering at that point.

Sound

Cardo partners with JBL. Sena has used Harman Kardon across the 50 and 60 series, and the newest 60S EVO moves to Bose.

In practice both are far better than they need to be for talking, and the difference only shows up with music at highway speed. Cardo's 45 mm speakers on the Packtalk Pro are the biggest drivers in either lineup and it's audible. Below that tier, the two brands trade blows and your helmet matters more than your intercom — a quiet touring helmet with deep ear pockets will beat a loud helmet with better speakers every time.

Don't buy on audio unless you listen to a lot of music at speed. If you do, go up a tier rather than switching brands.

How you control it

This is a real difference and it doesn't show up in specs.

Cardo is built voice-first. Say "Hey Cardo" and tell it what you want, and you rarely touch the unit. When it works it's genuinely better than fumbling for a button at 70 mph. When there's wind noise or you're wearing earplugs, it hears you less well.

Sena leans on the jog dial — a physical wheel you spin and click. It's fast, it's tactile, and it works exactly the same every time. The catch is winter gloves and off-road gauntlets, where a wheel is harder to operate than a button. That's why Sena also sells button-based versions of the same units: the 50R is a 50S with buttons, and the Spider RT1 is the ST1 with buttons.

If you ride in cold weather or off-road, weight this heavily. If you ride in mild conditions with thin gloves, Cardo's voice control is the nicer experience.

Mounting

Cardo's Packtalk Pro and Edge use a magnetic Air Mount. The unit snaps on and pulls off with one gloved hand, which matters more than it sounds like it should — it's the difference between charging your unit at a hotel and leaving your helmet on the nightstand with a cable running out of it.

The Packtalk Neo drops the magnetic mount to hit a lower price. Sena uses clamp mounts across the line.

If you move one unit between two helmets, or you charge it nightly on tour, the Edge's mount is worth the money over the Neo. If your unit lives on one helmet and gets charged in the garage, it's not.

What each brand has that the other doesn't

Cardo: crash detection

The Packtalk Pro detects a crash and automatically alerts an emergency contact. If you ride alone, or ride remote, that's a genuine safety feature and it's the reason to pay the extra over an Edge. If you always ride with people, it's less compelling.

Sena: WAVE

The 60S and 60S EVO add WAVE, which routes intercom over the cellular network instead of radio. That removes the range limit entirely — you can talk to a rider who's an hour ahead of you. It's the most genuinely new thing either brand has shipped in years, and it only matters if you actually ride separated from your group.

Price

At list price, the two brands sit close. In practice they don't, because Sena discounts far more aggressively and more often. As of the last update, most of the Sena line was 25% off while Cardo's flagships held at full price.

That means the honest price comparison changes month to month. A Sena 50S at $284 against a Packtalk Edge at $440 is a very different decision than the same two at list.

Check the live price before you decide. Both brands run frequent sitewide promotions, and the gap between them swings by well over a hundred dollars depending on when you look.

So which one

  • Your group already runs one brand. Buy that brand. Nothing else on this page matters.
  • Starting fresh, want the best experience. Cardo Packtalk Edge. The mount, the voice control, and the mesh that reconnects without you noticing.
  • Starting fresh, want the most for the money. Sena 50S. Same mesh network as Sena's flagship, frequently well under $300.
  • You ride alone in remote places. Cardo Packtalk Pro for crash detection, or Sena 60S for WAVE. Different answers to the same fear.
  • Heavy gloves, cold weather, off-road. Sena 50R or Spider RT1 — buttons beat both a jog dial and voice control when your hands are cold.
  • Just you and a passenger. Neither. You don't need mesh at all — read this first and save yourself two hundred dollars.
