Cardo Packtalk Neo review

The same mesh network as Cardo's $500 flagship for around $252. There's exactly one thing missing, and whether it matters comes down to how you charge your unit.

The short version

$359.95 list, and Cardo has been discounting it to around $252. You get second-generation Dynamic Mesh, JBL 40 mm speakers, 13 hours of talk time and IP67 waterproofing — identical to the Packtalk Edge in every one of those. What you lose is the magnetic Air Mount. If your unit lives on one helmet, this is the smartest buy in Cardo's lineup.

The network is not a compromise

This is the thing to understand before anything else. The Neo runs the same second-generation DMC as the Packtalk Pro and Packtalk Edge. Not a reduced version, not a smaller group limit — the same network.

It groups with Pros and Edges with no compromise on either side, gets the same free Mesh Boost update that raised group capacity and added public mesh and remote grouping, and claims the same 1.6 km rider-to-rider range.

Speakers are the same JBL 40 mm drivers as the Edge. Battery is the same 13 hours. Waterproofing is the same IP67. Voice control is the same "Hey Cardo" system.

You are not buying a lesser mesh experience. You're buying a lesser mount.

The mount, and whether you'll care

The Packtalk Edge and Pro use a magnetic Air Mount. The unit snaps on and pulls off in one motion, with gloves, in about two seconds.

The Neo uses a conventional clamp. To charge it, you remove the whole bracket from the helmet rather than lifting the unit off a base plate.

How much that matters splits cleanly:

  • You'll barely notice if the unit stays on one helmet and you charge it in the garage with a cable, or overnight where the helmet sits.
  • You'll notice constantly if you move one unit between a street helmet and a dirt helmet, or if you tour and take the unit inside to charge every night.

That's the whole decision. At roughly $190 apart, it's a lot of money for a mount — but it's a mount you touch every single day.

What it beats

The comparison that actually matters isn't Neo against Edge. It's Neo against everything else at its price.

Around $250, most of what's available is Bluetooth: Cardo's own Freecom 4X, Sena's SF4 and 5S. Those are fine units and completely wrong for a group of three or more, because a Bluetooth chain breaks every time somebody drops out.

Sena's Spider ST1 and Spider RT1 undercut the Neo on price and are genuine mesh. Sena's 50S at sale price sits close and adds Harman Kardon audio.

Against those, the Neo's argument is Cardo's voice control and the DMC network — and, if your friends run Packtalks, the fact that it joins them.

What's missing compared to the flagships

  • No magnetic mount — the main one.
  • No crash detection — Packtalk Pro only.
  • No auto on/off — Packtalk Pro only.
  • 40 mm speakers rather than 45 mm — same as the Edge, so not a step down from it.

Who should buy it

  • Buy the Neo if you want Cardo mesh, ride with a group, and keep the unit on one helmet.
  • Buy the Packtalk Edge if you swap helmets or charge on the road. Full review.
  • Buy the Packtalk Pro if you ride alone and want crash detection. Comparison.
  • Look at Sena instead if price is the binding constraint — the Spider ST1 goes lower. Review here.
Outfitting a whole group? The Neo is where the maths gets interesting. Four Packtalk Edges is about $1,760. Four Neos is closer to $1,010, and everyone's on the identical network. That's a set of tires' worth of difference for a mount.
