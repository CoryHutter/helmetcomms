Helmets with built-in comms pockets

A growing number of helmets have a dedicated cavity that only accepts one specific unit. Cleaner install, better aerodynamics, and no universal option — so the helmet decides the intercom.

The short version

If your helmet has a comms cavity, the matched unit is almost always the right buy: it sits flush, doesn't spoil the aerodynamics, and installs without fighting the liner. The catch is that it locks you to one brand's network, so check what your riding group runs before you commit to the helmet, not after.

What a comms cavity actually is

A universal intercom clamps to the outside of the shell. It works on anything, and it adds a lump to the side of your helmet that catches wind and looks like an accessory.

A helmet with a built-in cavity has a recess moulded into the shell, sized for a specific unit. The unit drops into it and sits flush. The helmet keeps its intended aerodynamics, its noise profile, and the safety characteristics it was certified with — all things a clamp-on can affect.

The trade is obvious: that recess fits one unit. There's no universal option and no swapping brands later.

Shoei

The most established of these, and now served by both brands.

Cardo makes the Packtalk-S and 4X-S for Shoei's third-generation helmets — the GT-Air 3, Neotec 3 and J-Cruise 3. They fit into the helmet's designated communications cavity rather than clamping on, keeping the profile clean while preserving the helmet's aerodynamics and comfort.

Sena makes the SRL2 for Shoei helmets, and the SRL-EXT specifically for the RF-1400.

So a Shoei owner genuinely has a choice — which is unusual, and useful if your group has already settled on a brand.

HJC

Cardo makes the HJC 4X and HJC Spirit, integrated units for HJC helmets with a comms pocket. Same principle: flush fit, no external hardware.

Cardo's own helmets

Cardo now sells helmets directly — the Beyond and the Venture — designed around their comms from the start rather than accommodating them afterwards. The Venture is aimed at adventure and off-road riding.

Obviously these tie you to Cardo entirely. If your group runs Cardo anyway, that's not a cost.

The UCS standard

Worth knowing about, because it may make this whole page obsolete eventually.

Cardo's Universal Communication System is an attempt to standardise the size and shape of the cavity across manufacturers, so helmets built to ECE 22.06 can accept an integrated unit without every helmet needing its own bespoke version.

Cardo has OEM relationships across a broad range of manufacturers — Harley-Davidson, KTM, Honda, HJC, Schuberth, Leatt, Simpson, Ruroc and others — so the standard has real backing. Whether it becomes genuinely universal or stays a Cardo-shaped standard is an open question.

Should you buy the integrated unit?

Yes, if

  • You already own the helmet and it has the cavity
  • You care about wind noise and aerodynamics — a clamp-on unit measurably affects both
  • You want a clean install without wrestling a clamp between shell and liner
  • Your riding group already runs the matching brand

No, if

  • You move one unit between multiple helmets — an integrated unit lives in one helmet, permanently
  • Your group runs the other brand. The helmet doesn't get to make this decision for you; the networks don't connect
  • You want the flagship features. Integrated units generally track a specific model in the range, so check what you're actually getting before assuming it matches the top unit

The order to decide in

People get this backwards. They buy the helmet, then discover the integrated unit is the wrong brand for their group.

The right order:

  • First, find out what your regular riding group runs.
  • Second, if you're buying a helmet with a cavity, check which brand's unit it takes.
  • Third, if those two don't match, either pick a different helmet or accept a clamp-on unit in a helmet designed not to need one.

The third option isn't a disaster — a universal unit works on any helmet, cavity or not. You just paid for a feature you can't use.

If your helmet has no cavity

Most don't, and that's completely fine. Every universal unit in both catalogues clamps or adheres to any helmet. Check the install guide for getting the speakers and microphone in the right place, which matters far more to your experience than whether the unit sits flush.
