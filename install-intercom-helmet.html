How to install an intercom in your helmet

An hour the first time, twenty minutes after that. Most of the job is getting two things in the right place — and those two things are what people get wrong.

The short version

Speakers go directly over your ear canals, not centred in the pockets. The microphone goes at the corner of your mouth, not in front of it. Everything else is cable routing. Test the fit before you put the liner back, because that's the part nobody wants to redo.

Before you start

Set aside an hour, work somewhere with good light, and charge the unit while you go — you'll want it powered for the test.

Take the cheek pads and liner out of your helmet first. Most modern helmets have snap-in liners, and everything is easier with them out. Take a photo before you pull anything, so you know how it went back together.

Worth knowing first: Cardo has an arrangement with Cycle Gear for free installation with an online purchase. If you're not confident, that's a real option and it costs nothing. Some dealers will fit one for you too — worth asking.

Step 1: choose clamp or adhesive

Every kit ships with both.

Clamp mount slides between the shell and the liner and grips the edge. Removable, reusable on another helmet, and the better choice if there's a gap to slide it into. Some tight-fitting helmets simply don't have that gap.

Adhesive plate sticks to the outside of the shell. Works on any helmet including ones with no gap, but it's a commitment — that helmet now owns the mount.

Try the clamp first. If it won't seat without forcing, switch to adhesive.

Position it below the visor line so it doesn't interfere with the visor's travel, and on the side you'd naturally reach with your free hand — left for most riders.

Step 2: speakers, and the mistake everyone makes

This is the step that determines whether you're happy with the unit.

Position the speaker over your ear canal, not centred in the pocket. The two are rarely the same spot. Helmet ear pockets are cut for airflow and shell shape; your ear is where it is. A speaker two centimetres off-target sounds thin, quiet and disappointing, and no amount of volume fixes it.

The way to get it right: put the helmet on with speakers loosely placed, and move them until the sound is directly in your ears. Then mark the position before you commit the velcro.

If they press on your ears, your pockets are too shallow for standard drivers. Use the slim speaker option if the kit includes one, or the supplied spacer pads to build the surrounding area up rather than the speaker itself. Pressure that's mild in the driveway becomes painful by hour three.

Route the speaker cable around the back of the helmet under the liner, keeping it flat. A cable that sits proud will press into your head.

Step 3: microphone

Which mic you use depends on your helmet.

Full-face helmets take the wired microphone, tucked behind the chin bar padding.

Modular, open-face and MX helmets take the boom microphone, on its arm.

Position it at the corner of your mouth, not directly in front. Straight in front of your lips catches your breath, and every time you exhale the noise cancelling has to fight it. Off to the corner picks up your voice and misses the airflow.

Most microphones have a marked side that must face your mouth. Look for the dot or the label — a reversed mic technically works and sounds terrible, and it's a common cause of "nobody can hear me."

If you use a boom mic, aim it just off the corner, roughly a thumb's width from your lips.

Step 4: test before you reassemble

Do not skip this. Put the liner back loosely, fit the unit, and:

  • Play music. Is it centred in both ears, or off to one side?
  • Make a call or use the app's mic test. Can they hear you clearly?
  • Wear it for ten minutes. Any pressure point at all will be worse at hour three.

Fixing a speaker position now takes two minutes. Fixing it after everything's snapped back together takes twenty.

Step 5: reassemble and pair

Snap the liner and cheek pads back in, checking no cable is trapped or pinched. Then pair your phone, and pair with the riders you'll be with — mesh grouping is done once and remembered afterwards.

Update the firmware before your first ride. Both brands push updates over the air through their apps, and on Sena in particular, mismatched mesh versions between riders will stop a group working.

Common problems

  • Sound is thin or quiet. Speakers aren't over your ear canals. Move them, don't turn it up.
  • Nobody can hear you. Microphone is reversed, or sitting in front of your mouth instead of at the corner.
  • Ears hurt after an hour. Pockets too shallow. Slim speakers or spacer pads.
  • Wind noise on your voice. Move the mic further into the corner, and check the foam cover is fitted.
  • Clamp won't fit. Use the adhesive plate. Clean the shell with the supplied wipe first and give it a day before you ride.
